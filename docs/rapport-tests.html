Pokémon TCG Scanner · test report · 6 August 2026

The recognition works. It is the framing that decides.

Fifteen photos taken with an iPhone were run through the full chain. The embedding model is never the limiting factor: on a correct, upright crop, the right card comes out on top eleven times out of twelve. All the rest of the work happens before it.

What was built

20,444cards in the base, 174 sets through July 2026
20,394high-resolution reference images, 5.5 GB
42 MBembedding index, 21 MB in float16 for iOS
38 msper identification: 34 ms of embedding, 4 ms of search

The index is built with MobileCLIP2-S2, the model Apple designs for its Neural Engine, in 512-dimension vectors. Its full construction takes six minutes on the M3 Pro. The search is done by matrix product over the 20,394 vectors, without FAISS — at this size it is exact and instant, and above all it is identical to what the app will do via Accelerate.

Fifty cards have no image available on the official CDN (McDonald's promos), i.e. 0.24 % of the base.

The test protocol

Fifteen real photos, taken with an iPhone in deliberately ordinary conditions: rattan table, cables, laptop screen, backlight, cards held in the hand or in a sleeve. All the cards are French whereas the index is in English.

The ground truth was established by reading the name, the number and the set code printed on each card, not by trusting the system. Two cards turned out to be absent from the index — their promo set is not covered by the data source — and are excluded from the calculation rather than counted as failures. A sixteenth photo, showing five cards spread out, is used for the multi-card test.

The gap that matters

Between the current 7/12 and the 11/12 of the ceiling, there is no change of model, no retraining, no additional data. Only the choice, among the crops already computed, of the right one. The project's most important gain today is a question of selection logic.

The gap between the first and the second candidate predicts correctness almost perfectly. Sorted by decreasing margin, the first six identifications are all correct.

The absolute score, for its part, discriminates nothing. The worst failure of the set — Mega Blastoise ex, outside the top-5 — shows the second-best score of all the photos, at 0.8598. Trusting it would have produced a false certainty. The reason is structural: all cards share a layout, so two entirely unrelated cards already resemble each other at about 0.79. The useful scale is 0.79 to 1, not 0 to 1.

Failure diagnosis

Sorted by measured impact, not by supposed difficulty. It is this list that should fix the order of the work.

1

The card is rectified upside down

Four of the five failures are on a crop flipped 180°. Inspecting the retained crops, the right orientation existed every time and got a good score — it simply lost the arbitration. Eleven of the twelve ideal variants are at 0°: the rectification therefore already produces the card the right way up in almost every case.

Simply discarding the 180° variant takes it from 7/12 to 9/12, but would break a card actually photographed upside down. The clean solution is to read the direction of the text on the crop.

measured gain 7/12 → 9/12 ceiling 11/12
2

The wrong quadrilateral is retained

Vision proposes up to nine rectangles per photo: the card, but also a table slab, a screen, a bit of keyboard. The current rule retains the one that maximizes score + margin, which is sometimes fooled by a plain crop — a fragment of table stands out clearly against an Energy card, itself almost plain.

An aspect-ratio constraint would help: a card is 63 × 88 mm, i.e. a ratio of 0.716, and the artefact crops often depart from it.

remaining gain 9/12 → 11/12
3

Two cards too visually close

Cryogonal 209/193 confused with Baxcalibur 210/193: two consecutive illustration rares from the same set, same style, the right answer at rank 2 with a 0.006 gap. Same pattern for Golisopod, the only case that stays wrong even with a perfect choice of crop.

This is the real limit of the global embedding, and the only point on this list that requires genuine modelling work. The planned lead stays re-ranking on the bottom strip, which carries the number and the set symbol — that is precisely what distinguishes these cards, and the reason the high-resolution images were chosen.

irreducible without re-ranking
4

Overlapping cards are not separated

On a photo of five cards where four overlap, only one was isolated — the only well-separated one — but it was correctly identified. Vision needs to see the four edges of a closed rectangle.

For a collection scan, you will have to either ask for the cards to be spaced out, or train the dedicated detector planned as plan B. It is the only question this test set answers in the negative.

1 card detected out of 5
5

Cards absent from the index

Two cards out of fourteen belong to a promo set the data source does not cover. Their artwork was compared to every indexed variant: no match. No model can find them.

This is not a recognition problem but a data-freshness one, to watch on every set release.

2 cards out of 14 not findable

What the tests settled

  • French cards are recognized from an English index. Funécire comes out as Litwick at 0.873, Superdofin as Palafin at 0.871. The artwork is identical from one language to the other and weighs far more than the text. A per-language index is not needed.
  • Apple's detector is enough for an isolated card. It finds the card on all fifteen photos, where the home-grown detector missed three. The dedicated detection model considered at the start is only needed for the multi-card case.
  • Full-art holos are not a weak point. A first reading blamed them; inspecting the crops showed their failures came from the orientation. The card that gets the best margin of the whole set is itself a full-art.
  • The embedding model does not need to be changed for now. The ceiling of 11/12 is reached with MobileCLIP2-S2 as is. Comparing other models before the framing is fixed would amount to optimizing what does not limit.

What stays open

  • Twelve photos is few. Gaps of one or two photos are not significant, and the 0.04 margin threshold needs to be confirmed on a larger sample before being wired into the app.
  • No card reprinted across several sets was tested. Yet it is the confusion case identified from the start, measured at a 0.021 gap between the Base Pikachu and its Base Set 2 reprint.
  • No old card was tested. All the photos are of recent cards; the layouts of the Base, Neo or EX series are very different.
  • Nothing has run on an iPhone yet. Vision was driven from Python via PyObjC, which validates the API but not the on-device performance or the Core ML conversion of the embedding model.

Measurements reproducible with scripts/evaluate_real.py. The ground truth of the photos is in data/eval/truth.json, established by reading the cards.
