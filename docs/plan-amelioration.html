Pokémon TCG Scanner · improvement plan · 6 August 2026

Five tasks, no retraining.

This plan follows from the test report and a second batch of seven photos that brings the set to 21. The measured ceiling is 17/18 with the current model: the whole plan aims to close the gap between today's 13/18 and that ceiling, then to make reliable what the ceiling does not cover — the card's exact edition.

Three new facts reorient the plan relative to the initial report.

  • The 180° variant genuinely serves a purpose. Three correct photos of the set (5031, 5032, 5037) were only identified thanks to it: the rectification sometimes puts the card upside down. The "remove the 180° variant" trick measured at +2 on the first batch would have broken those three. The real solution is to read the direction of the text.
  • A low margin does not always mean "wrong": it often means "right card, uncertain edition". Primeape is correct at a 0.0304 margin — its only competitors are other Primeapes from other sets. Water Energy is identified at 0.0159 — all the candidates are Water Energies. The confusion is concentrated almost entirely within the same card name.
  • The bottom-strip OCR works, tested with the exact iOS API. VNRecognizeTextRequest in fast mode reads "043/084" and "152/132" (with 7↔1 confusions that are correctable, since the valid number/total pairs are known). And it reads 6 strings the right way up against 1 to 2 upside down: the volume of recognized text is on its own an orientation detector.

The tasks, by gain/effort ratio

A

Orientation by text reading

On each rectified crop, run a fast text recognition on both orientations and keep the one that reads the most strings. Measured on three cards: 6 strings the right way up against 1–2 upside down, without exception. Estimated cost 10–20 ms, once per tracked card — not per frame.

Replaces the current score+margin arbitration between 0° and 180°, responsible for the three failures 5018, 5020 and 5030 (and the rank 2 of 5024).

Exit criterion: the three orientation failures move to top-1 without breaking 5031, 5032 or 5037. Expected: 13/18 → 16/18.

expected gain +3 de-risked by OCR test effort: low
B

Filtering the quadrilaterals before embedding

The right crop exists in 17 cases out of 18; when it loses, it is against an artefact. Three filters before encoding: short/long side ratio close to 0.716 (a card is 63 × 88 mm), minimum area, and rejection of near-plain crops (low pixel variance — a bit of table contains no artwork). Fewer candidates also means fewer embeddings to compute per frame.

Exit criterion: reach the ceiling of 17/18, and halve the number of encoded variants.

expected gain +1 effort: low
C

Two-level confidence: the name, then the edition

Wire the product decision on two distinct margins. The classic margin (1st vs 2nd candidate) validates the edition; the margin to the first candidate of a different name validates the identity. On the current set, a threshold of 0.03 on the first gives 100 % precision, and the second additionally recovers the "right card, ambiguous edition" cases like Primeape.

On the interface side: above both thresholds, firm result; identity sure but edition ambiguous, display "Primeape — edition to confirm"; below, top-5 or a request to steady the shot. Never display the absolute score, which discriminates nothing.

Exit criterion: zero wrong identification displayed as firm on the test set, thresholds reconfirmed on at least 40 photos.

product reliability effort: low
D

Reading the bottom strip: exact edition and cards out of index

On the validated crop, OCR of the bottom strip ("043/084", set code) and fuzzy matching against the known number/total pairs — the observed 7↔1 and 2↔) confusions are corrected because the space of valid values is closed. It is the reason the high-resolution images were downloaded in phase 1.

Settles four problems at once: the set's only irreducible failure (Golisopod, rank 2 against an identical artwork), the reprints at a 0.02 gap like Base Pikachu / Base Set 2, the set attribution of Energy cards, and the detection of cards out of index — a read number that matches no known card of the set signals "unknown card" instead of a confident wrong attribution.

Exit criterion: correct edition on the set's low-margin cases, and MEG 003 Energy flagged as out of index instead of attributed to Paldea Evolved.

settles the ceiling's last failure OCR precision to confirm in accurate mode effort: medium
E

Fill and refresh the index

Three photographed cards out of twenty-one are absent from pokemon-tcg-data: two "MEP" promos and the base Energy of the Mega Evolution sets. Evaluate a complementary source for the recent promos and energies (the pokemontcg.io API directly, or TCGdex which covers European releases well), then automate the chain already in place — metadata, images, embeddings, export — into a single job to rerun on every set release.

Exit criterion: the three absent cards become identifiable; full rebuild of the index in one command.

3/21 not findable today effort: medium

What we are not doing, and why

  • Changing the embedding model. The ceiling of 17/18 is reached with MobileCLIP2-S2 as is. Comparing DINOv2 or another encoder before tasks A and B are exhausted would amount to optimizing what does not limit — to be re-tested only if the ceiling holds.
  • Training a YOLO now. A single photo of the set would require it (five overlapping cards). The "collection scan" mode can start with a spacing instruction; the dedicated detector stays plan B, triggered by real use, not by anticipation.
  • Building a per-language index. Eighteen identifiable French cards out of eighteen were identified from the English index. Task D reinforces this point further by relying on the numbers, identical from one language to the other.
  • Training a classifier. Still ruled out: the detection + embedding + search architecture absorbs a new set by a simple index rebuild (task E).

Sequence and switch to the app

Tasks A, B and C are independent, small, and all validate against the same test bench (evaluate_real.py): it is the first salvo, in Python. D follows, since it depends on the crop stabilized by A and B. E advances in parallel, it is data, not recognition code.

The switch to the iOS app (Core ML conversion, photo-to-card app of the initial plan) triggers when two conditions are met: 16/18 or better on the test bench, and the Core ML parity verified (similarity > 0.99 between the PyTorch embedding and the converted embedding). The whole pipeline validated here — Vision, rectification, OCR — already uses the phone's APIs: the port is a translation, not a reinvention.

The main risk

Eighteen photos remain a small sample, concentrated on recent cards from one player. The thresholds (0.03 margin, text volume for the orientation) must be reconfirmed as we go: every new batch of photos goes through the test bench before adjusting anything. Still missing: old cards (Base, Neo, EX), very damaged cards, and the same reprinted Pokémon photographed in two different sets.
